**Title**

    What a Number Looks Like in Four Bits

**Description**

    A floating-point number is three parts: a sign, an exponent, and a mantissa. FP32 spends 32 bits on it. FP8 splits the difference two ways. FP4 gets down to four bits: one sign, two exponent, one mantissa.

    One mantissa bit is not enough on its own. The block scale is what saves the numbers, and it changes for every group of values.

**Title**

    Why a Language Model Can Write Out of Order

**Description**

    Every language model you have used writes left to right, one token at a time. A diffusion model does not generate — it denoises, unmasking the slots it is confident about, in any order.

    The speedup is real. The catch is that the tokens are not equally confident, and every slot you unmask early is one you cannot take back.

**Title**

    Same Sixteen Bits, Different Split

**Description**

    BF16 has the same sixteen bits as FP16 and a completely different number: eight exponent bits and seven mantissa bits, against five and ten.

    It keeps FP32's range and throws away precision, which is exactly what deep learning needed. It also makes the conversion to FP32 a bit shift, with no rounding and no range check.
